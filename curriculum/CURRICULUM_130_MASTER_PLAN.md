**These are planned first-introduction slots, not actual introduced, mastered or spoken words.** The **1,500 minimum is a hard curriculum deliverable**: we must not declare completion of Book II if fewer than 1,500 distinct, verified lemma IDs have actually been introduced in its Latin texts. The same explicit audit rule applies to the 3,000+ end-of-course stretch.

The earlier user decision to keep approximately 15 new words per chapter remains in force for the **first 50 chapters**. Later reader levels have longer reading units and intentionally higher budgets, not a sudden vocabulary flood in the beginner chapters. If these targets prove too steep in pilot lessons, expand the number of chapters; never manufacture vocabulary counts or weaken the beginner progression.

## Why this is modelled on Ørberg (without copying)

The publisher describes `Familia Romana` as 35 connected Roman-family chapters teaching almost 1,800 words through repeated Latin contextual reading, two or three lectiones per chapter, images, marginal notes, a grammar consolidation section and three Pensa. The more advanced `Roma Aeterna` includes increasingly complex classical material and approximately 3,000 vocabulary words. The publisher does not give a chapter-by-chapter acquisition schedule, and these counts are not evidence of automatically achieved proficiency. 

**Tablinum uses the pedagogical pattern, never Ørberg's protected text or images:**
1. A story and illustration introduce a few forms in an understandable scene.
2. Read the Latin *lectio* before revealing English; Latin-only margin cues give an example, contrast, synonym or clear picture.
3. Reuse the new word in different contexts; aim for 8–10 meaningful encounters across the chapter's story, dialogue and activities, then recycle it later.
4. Reveal a compact grammar explanation *after* seeing the pattern several times; English grammar details remain optional.
5. After **each lectio**, check contextual comprehension and one focused form; after the chapter, ask an original integrated exercise set.
6. Reread after feedback; add listening, shadowing and retelling as **separate** skills, not presumed by silent reading.
7. Add a **zero-new-lemma review story** roughly every fifth chapter.
8. Progress to original longer narratives, and finally to properly sourced, annotated real Latin selections.

The working Manuscript Edition remains offline/local-first. Heavy morphology, alignment, lexical data checking and licensing analysis occur offline, with small verified JSON assets delivered to the app when implemented.

## Seven content arcs

### Book I: concrete scenes and first forms (Chapters 1–10)

Point, name, give, eat and count; make familiar Latin forms meaningful before grammar tables.

### Book I: dialogue, movement and past descriptions (Chapters 11–25)

Introduce first/second-person forms, locations, adjectives, plurals, imperatives and imperfect gently.

### Book I: tenses, clauses and original bridges (Chapters 26–50)

Build short narratives, passive and common subordinate constructions, revisiting earlier scenes.

### Book II: farms, roads and spoken problem-solving (Chapters 51–70)

Deepen case meaning, time, quantity, cause, conditionals and indirect questions; grow oral dialogue.

### Book II: cities, travel, messages and reports (Chapters 71–90)

More extended literary-style prose with complex clauses, reported speech and review mysteries.

### Book III: original novella and cultural narrative (Chapters 91–110)

Longer, partially unassisted reading with nuanced dialogue and sustained speaking tasks.

### Book III: fables, history and transition to authors (Chapters 111–130)

Interpret perspectives and rhetoric, retell extended stories, then bridge to legally sourced authentic texts.


### Review-cycle v2: grammar prerequisites and corrected Latin titles

Claude's review found that the original 130-chapter draft taught some advanced constructions before their prerequisites. The v2 revisions are listed in [CLAUDE_REVIEW_RESPONSE_V2.md](CLAUDE_REVIEW_RESPONSE_V2.md) alongside the exact source review and remaining pedagogical risks.

**Key dependency repairs:**

- **Chapters 34–36:** third-declension nouns → third-declension adjectives (*omnis, fortis, celer*) → simple subject relative clauses. Demonstratives are now taught in Chapter 71, with oblique relative pronouns in Chapter 72.
- **Chapters 38–44 and 51–74:** simple passive/participles → future active participle (43) → deponents (44) → ablative means/cause/accompaniment (51, 52, 57) → imperfect/future/pluperfect passive (59, 61, 64) → **ablative absolute in Chapter 73**.
- **Chapters 50 and 67–79:** the first *lectio* in Chapter 50 presents present-subjunctive forms, followed by **purpose clauses with *ut* and *nē***. Chapter 67 introduces the primary-sequence perfect subjunctive; Chapter 68 makes primary/secondary sequence explicit with reported commands. Chapter 74 uses *cum* + **imperfect** subjunctive; Chapter 79 introduces the pluperfect subjunctive in a past unreal conditional, so the more difficult *cum* + pluperfect is deferred until after that.
- **Other added topics:** superlatives (47, immediately after comparatives), vocative (54), fifth declension (56), formation of adverbs (58), impersonal *licet/oportet* (62), fear clauses (76), frequent irregular verbs (88), and restricted supine recognition (107).
- **Chapter 40** no longer places *sequor* in its story before deponents are introduced in Chapter 44.
- **Chapter I–X reading targets have increased to 250–450 running tokens**, with two or three lectiones and contextual practice; subsequent lengths grow gradually. Exposure counts may include original story sentences, Latin picture labels, comprehension questions and drills, but not English translation or duplicate flashcard display.

**Lexeme budget reconciliation:** the foundation allocates **500 planned first-introduction slots**, while the **570 figure is only the theoretical ceiling** (38 teaching chapters × max 15). The 50-chapter outline and core-list seed-audit now distinguish the two. The unchanged vocab milestones remain 500, 1,532 and 3,036 planned entries, not verified introduced vocabulary.

**Not fixed in this planning cycle:** a playable 130-chapter course, quality-checked original Latin stories, morphological lemmatization, human pronunciation/audio, offline lesson compilation, or legacy chapter progress migration. These are explicit future development gates; no claim of working software for new chapters is made.


### Claude's follow-up review — outline v3 (5 November 2026)

The latest review found that **pronoun teaching remained insufficient** and a few command/indirect-statement lessons were incorrectly placed or duplicated. We revised the 130-row machine-readable outline and this table together:

- **Chapter XI:** first- and second-person dialogue now contextualises `mē` and `tē` alongside `ego/tū`.
- **Chapter XV:** near/far demonstratives `hic/ille` are introduced with visible people and their nominative plurals (`hī/illī`); Chapter LXXI explicitly **consolidates their oblique-case paradigms** rather than introducing them for the first time.
- **Chapter XIV:** a staged mini-lectio teaches possessive adjectives `meus/mea/meum` and `tuus/tua/tuum` alongside singular adjective agreement.
- **Chapter XVI:** `nōs/vōs` appear as subjects and objects with the plural verb pattern.
- **Chapter XVIII:** vocative direct address (`Mārce!`, `Tīte!`) appears at the first positive commands, not first in Chapter LIV.
- **Chapter XX:** the **main grammar topic** is the third-person anaphoric pronoun `is/ea/id` and its forms `eum/eam/eius`, with an illustrated distinction between a person and their possessions.
- **Chapter XXVI:** one explicitly identified *secondary* pronoun mini-lectio contrasts `sē` and reflexive possessive `suus` with `eius`; its **primary topic remains the regular perfect**. This is a deliberate small dual-topic compromise that still needs a Latinist and teaching-pacing review.
- **Chapter LIV:** plural commands `venīte, audīte, portāte` replace a repeated first introduction of the vocative; vocative is reviewed in context.
- **Chapter LXXXI:** the indirect-statement present infinitive learned in Chapter XLII is **reused**, not reintroduced. The new focus is `eum/eam` (another person) versus `sē` (the reported speaker).
- **Macron corrections:** titles in Chapters LXXVI, LXXVIII, LXXXV, XCIV and CXX, plus recurring lemma spellings `teneō`, `sedeō`, `maneō`, `surgō`, `scrībō`, `sentiō`, `labōrō`.
- **Words/inflections:** the required forms in the JSON are **planned instructional examples**, not counted as independently introduced lexemes. `is/eum/eius` is one lexical headword, `suus/sua/suum` another; accents and morphological variants cannot artificially increase the 1,500/3,000 targets.

No new story text or audio has been written. All curriculum milestones are **budget allocations**, not audited evidence of lexical introduction. See `CLAUDE_REVIEW_RESPONSE_V3.md` and `validate-outline-v3.cjs`; do not approve actual Latin content based solely on JSON strings.

## Reading volume, hint design and oral competence

| Stage | Typical passage per chapter (running word tokens) | Reading segments | Learner action |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Early foundation, I–X | 250–450 | 2–3 | Identify objects; repeat a Latin phrase; answer a yes/no question. New words also recur in picture labels, story Q&A and drills. |
| Late foundation, XI–L | 420–1,050 | 3 | Answer who/where/why, follow directions, perform simple dialogue, retell 3–6 sentences |
| Developing, LI–XC | 800–2,400 | 4–5 | Describe pictures spontaneously, narrate events in past/future, deliver a message, role-play requests |
| Advanced, XCI–CXXX | 1,800–4,000 | 5–6 | Summarize 1–2 pages, explain cause and viewpoint, respond to follow-up questions, discuss a reading |

Lengths are **ranges for original content**, not promises of a single exact size; review chapters can be longer. A chapter with 40–49 new lemmas must provide enough prose and extra practice to revisit them naturally, rather than turning into a list of 49 unexplained words.

### Skill loop repeated in every chapter

- **Interpretive:** picture/context prediction → 2–6 original lectiones → glossed or unassisted reread → comprehension without translating everything.
- **Pronunciation/listening:** consistent human-checked model audio when available; device TTS is labelled unverified; listen, repeat and compare.
- **Interpersonal:** short Latin question-and-answer (at first scripted, later open-ended), simulated shopkeeper/visitor and travel dialogue; the app should not pretend a pronunciation score is clinically or academically validated.
- **Presentational:** describe a scene, retell the story, then later give an explanation or argument in connected Latin.
- **Grammar:** see form in context → Latin-only note → targeted drill → reread → cumulative review.
- **Retention:** compare introduced lemmas, encountered lemmas, recognised lemmas and productively used lemmas; do not merge the counters.

### Proficiency checkpoints (outcomes are measured, not automatic)

- **Chapter 25:** Understand short familiar stories with strong visual support; respond to simple questions about family, actions, places and time.
- **Chapter 50:** Read connected original beginner prose with selective help; take part in a rehearsed practical exchange and narrate familiar events in short clauses.
- **Chapter 75:** Read longer graded prose and express causes, plans, opinions, questions and indirect information with help as needed.
- **Chapter 90 — 1,500 minimum:** Independently follow a longer graded chapter, answer new comprehension questions and take part in a multi-turn, bounded Latin discussion. Actual lexical inventory must list >=1,500 unique approved IDs before this milestone can be marked achieved.
- **Chapter 110:** Read sustained original narratives with relatively sparse support; retell and discuss a viewpoint without memorising a script.
- **Chapter 130 — 3,000 stretch:** Read longer adapted-register Latin and attempt carefully licensed classical selections with dictionary help; discuss familiar and studied topics in connected Latin. Spoken ability will generally lag reading, and 3,000 introduced lemmas does **not** guarantee conversational fluency.

Assess reading comprehension, listening comprehension and speaking separately. The word count is only one measure. A teacher or capable Latinist should calibrate grammar accuracy, morphology, pronunciation and plausibility of exercises.

## Lexical inventory: actual 1,500 and 3,000 audit

The complete *source lexicon* is **not ready yet**. The user's Dickinson CSV contains 997 records, including combined headwords, and cannot supply 3,000 unique lexemes on its own. Build an independently reviewed lexical ledger using:
- the DCC roughly 1,000 high-frequency lemmas as an important initial priority, with careful homograph/alias resolution;
- separately licensed and individually vetted additional words from frequency dictionaries, original stories and annotated source readings;
- source IDs, meanings, part of speech, macron review, first introduction chapter, all occurrences and exercise/review references.

**Count a lexeme only when:** it occurs in an approved Latin reading or dialogue, the sense is supported by contextual teaching, it has an audited ID, and subsequent exercises/reading revisit it. Do not count:
- mere mentions in the plan, answer keys alone or English glossary text;
- alternate inflected forms (e.g. *rosa* versus *rosam*) as separate new words;
- characters' proper names, unless deliberately studied as common lexical items;
- the same headword twice in different lessons;
- homographs as identical without lexical disambiguation (e.g. *edō* 'eat' and *ēdō* 'put forth').

`vocabulary-budgets-130.json` records **planned allocations only**. `vocabulary-introductions-130.json` starts empty and prevents false 'verified vocabulary' reports. The checker `validate-roadmap-130.cjs` has an optional future `--require-introduced-1500` or `--require-introduced-3000` mode which fails until actual reviewed lesson ledgers satisfy the milestone.

### Graduation caveat

Completing 130 chapters and reading 3,000 introduced lemmas aims at **developing independent Latin reading and basic active use**. It does not confer a CEFR level, a speaking credential, expert Classical pronunciation or automatic success on unadapted poetry. A teacher-checked diagnostic on unseen Latin, plus an actual spoken exchange, is the appropriate completion gate.
