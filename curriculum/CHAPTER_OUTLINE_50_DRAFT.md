# Tablinum — Revised 50-chapter Latin course outline (approval draft)

**Prepared 5 November 2026.** This supersedes the earlier 35-chapter proposal. It implements the explicit course-length and vocabulary-limit choice in the user-provided handover `TABLINUM_HANDOVER_FOR_GPT(2).md` (section 12). **Nothing in the runnable program, local learner state, original lesson stories, audio or manuscript UI was modified.**

## Decisions carried into this draft

- **50 chapters: 38 teaching chapters, 12 review chapters** (5, 9, 13, 17, 21, 25, 29, 33, 37, 41, 45, 49).
- **About 15 new lemmas maximum** per teaching chapter; review stories introduce **zero** new lemmas. A new word should recur roughly 8–10 times during its introduction and later in review stories.
- The upper-bound arithmetic is **38 × 15 = 570 new lemmas** inside the main course, consistent with the proposed 550–600. The long-term 800–1,200-word target requires graded additional readings and a bridge to adapted Aesop and *Fabulae Faciles*, not more words packed into early chapters.
- **Accusative before dative:** Chapter III is supper/direct objects; Chapter IV is a giving/recipient story, reversing the previous outline's order.
- Deliberately adds the requested grammar coverage: **ablative, first/second person, nominative plurals, adjective agreement, infinitive, pronouns, indirect statement, ablative absolute, deponents, comparatives and 4th/5th-declension nouns**.
- Old Chapter VI–VIII narratives are reworked gradually through the course (especially storm, imperfect, negative command and future formation). No existing content is deleted at this approval stage.
- Original Latin-first prose and watercolour-ink Roman illustrations remain the goal; English is opt-in only, with high-quality Latin-only contextual hints.

## How vocabulary was checked

The uploaded `latin-core-list.csv` has **997 source rows**, but some rows combine multiple headwords. The parsing pass treats semicolon-separated entries as searchable aliases while retaining their single original row. For example, `fīlia -ae f.; fīlius -ī m.` is **one DCC source entry**, and *fīlius* is now recognized from that entry. First-time DCC seed mentions total **144 distinct matching lemma keys**. 26 extra spelling/word forms did not exactly match an independent headword, so they are annotated for human review in the JSON audit.

Important editorial corrections:

1. Use **`edō` (eat)**, not the source CSV's homographic **`ēdō` (put forth / publish)**. Neither the normalisation algorithm nor a shared spelling is sufficient to merge these lexemes. `edō` for eat and `bibō` for drink are ordinary supplementary teaching words, not falsely represented as DCC matches.
2. Use **`bibō`**, not **`bībō`**, for *drink* in the lesson plan.
3. Use **`magnus`** consistently for *large*. The supplied CSV spells the corresponding core-list headword `māgnus -a -um`; that source spelling is retained in the audit as data but treated as a possible macron error. Human lexical review is required before importing it.
4. The core list includes **`fīlius`** inside the combined `fīlia; fīlius` row. The audit now recognizes it without double-counting the DCC entry.

## Editorial pacing and the remaining 15-word budget

The table deliberately does **not** fabricate exactly 15 "verified" new vocabulary words for each chapter: that requires writing original stories, reviewing inflections, counting repetitions and validating the lemma coverage. The audit records the currently proposed first-time DCC matches, reused words, non-core or unmatched words, and a maximum of 15 for each teaching chapter. The new-word allowance is **a ceiling, not a requirement**; early chapters may introduce fewer words.

Some advanced paradigms cannot all receive equal space by Chapter L. Proposed exceptions and later readings must revisit: **superlatives, future of īre, full demonstrative declension, more subjunctive uses and passive/participle variations**. Those topics must not be claimed as mastered merely because an example appears in the story. A Latinist should examine the proposed late-course sequence before writing it.
