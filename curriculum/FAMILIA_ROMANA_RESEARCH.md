# Research: what Tablinum can learn from *Lingua Latina per se illustrata*

**Researched 5 November 2026.** This is an original curriculum design review, not a transcription of Ørberg's books. Source links below are current publisher, classroom/practice, curriculum standards and scholarly vocabulary resources.

## A. Verified *Familia Romana* design

1. **Story continuity:** The publisher describes **35 chapters** telling the life of a second-century Roman family. Grammar and vocabulary are learned through a sustained, intelligible narrative rather than unrelated translation drills. Each chapter has **two or three lectiones**, followed by **Grammatica Latina** and **three Pensa**. There is also a vocabulary index, Roman calendar and morphology aids. [Hackett: Familia Romana](https://hackettpublishing.com/lingua-latina-per-se-illustrata-series/familia-romana-part-i/lingua-latina-pars-i-familia-romana-full-color-edition).
2. **Approximately 1,800 items in Book I:** Hackett advertises **almost 1,800 vocabulary words**, with creatively varied and frequent repetition. Dickinson College Commentaries independently cites **1,811 lemmas** for Ørberg from Muccigrosso (2004). These are source-reported counts, not a new count from the copyrighted text and not a guarantee of 1,800 mastered words. [Hackett](https://hackettpublishing.com/lingua-latina-per-se-illustrata-series/familia-romana-part-i/lingua-latina-pars-i-familia-romana-full-color-edition) · [DCC](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/vocab/core-vocabulary).
3. **Comprehensible, Latin-first presentation:** The publisher emphasizes long contextual reading, illustrations and explanatory margin notes that allow a learner to infer meaning before analytical grammar explanation. An English translation is not required as the default reading mode. [Hackett series](https://www.hackettpublishing.com/lingua-latina-per-se-illustrata-series).
4. **Reread and exercises after each segment:** Schola Latina's teaching guidance recommends rereading every chapter until comprehended, paying attention to pictures and margins, reading the grammar section, and answering supplementary exercises *after each lectio*, not merely after the whole chapter. This is practitioner advice, not a randomized comparative efficacy study. [Schola Latina method](https://scholalatina.it/en/latine-discere-aude-our-method/).
5. **The advanced transition:** *Roma Aeterna* progresses through increasingly demanding literary selections, beginning with adapted text and ultimately largely unadapted sources. Hackett lists approximately **3,000 vocabulary words** for this second volume. That figure must not be casually added to 1,800: how the publisher counts overlap is not established here. [Hackett: Roma Aeterna](https://hackettpublishing.com/classics/lingua-latina-pars-ii-roma-aeterna-second-edition-with-full-color-illustrations).

### What cannot be inferred from the sources

- We have **not** derived per-chapter lexical counts, exact repeated-word frequencies, vocabulary distributions, or an exhaustive chapter-by-chapter grammar map from the copyrighted book.
- The publisher's promotional description does not prove that a particular learner will speak spontaneously after completing the text.
- We will **not copy** Ørberg's Latin passages, characters, illustrations, marginal annotations, Pensa, or page compositions. Tablinum's stories, characters, prompts and artwork remain original.

## B. Research on vocabulary and reading

Dickinson's Latin Core Vocabulary list represents about **1,000 common headwords** and is reported to cover roughly **70% of running tokens** in a typical Latin text. The independent review could verify DCC's report that Diederich's 1,500-word list reached roughly **85%** coverage in a particular sample, but could **not verify the separate 1,500-lemma / 80% figure** in the linked page; that unsupported figure is omitted. These are **corpus- and genre-dependent estimates**, not an equivalence between word count and understanding. A learner with 1,500 words may still need abundant glosses when reading literature. The core list is **CC BY-SA 3.0**; verify redistribution obligations before bundling derived glosses. [DCC Latin core and methodology](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/vocab/core-vocabulary).

Our supplied DCC CSV has **997 rows**, including compound dictionary headwords (e.g., *fīlia; fīlius*). It is a **foundation for frequency prioritization, not a 3,000-word dictionary**. The remaining lemmas must come from newly reviewed supplementary lexical sources and from actually authored passages. Similar spelling does not establish identical lexical identity: *edō* (eat) and *ēdō* (give out/issue) must not merge.

## C. Reading competence versus speaking competence

The **Australian Curriculum: Latin** describes restored pronunciation, reading aloud, reciting and performing dialogues, backed by morphological understanding and cultural context. It also supports appropriate use of English for analysis and comparison. [Australian Curriculum Latin resource](https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/support-resources/learning-area-resources/latin_language_resource) · [Classical Languages framework](https://v8.australiancurriculum.edu.au/f-10-curriculum/languages/framework-for-classical-languages/?capability=ignore).

The American Classical League's standards identify interpretive (reading/listening), interpersonal and presentational communication as distinct skills. Reading progress **does not automatically guarantee conversational skill**; a curriculum that wants speaking must actually ask students to listen, respond, retell, describe and engage in interaction, with feedback. [Classical language standards](https://www.aclclassics.org/Portals/0/Site%20Documents/Publications/Standards_for_Classical_Language_Learning_2017%20FINAL.pdf) · [ACTFL modes](https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/world-readiness-standards-for-learning-languages/standards-summary) · [ACL/SCS Latin teacher preparation](https://www.aclclassics.org/Portals/0/Guidelines%20Latin%20Teacher%20Preparation%202023%20ACL-SCS.pdf).

**Tablinum therefore needs separate receptive and productive milestones.** Speech model quality must be human-checked; browser TTS remains labelled as a device voice. Classroom participation, actual speaking performance, retention and authentic-text reading are separate metrics, not automatically inferred from "3,000 introduced lemmas."

## D. Tablinum design decisions drawn from this research

| Observed approach | Tablinum adaptation (new, original design) |
|---|---|
| One continuous Roman-world story | Keep Mārcus, Flāvia, Līvia, Tītus and Canis through Foundation; develop new original arcs in books II–III |
| Two/three lectiones, growing in complexity | 2–3 short lectiones in Foundation, 3–5 in Developing Reader, 4–7 with optional graded side readings in Advanced |
| Visuals and marginal explanations | Original Roman ink-and-watercolour illustrations; clickable image-label hotspots; Latin contextual paraphrases, arrows, antonyms and optional English |
| Inductive reading with explicit consolidation | Teach forms in scene before a compact Latin grammar note; include an optional English explanation if requested |
| Frequent, varied revisiting | New lemmas used in several different sentences across reading, oral prompts and exercises; reappear in later chapters and review stories |
| Three types of chapter exercise plus ancillary practice | Vocabulary-in-context, morphological completion, comprehension; also true/false, sequence cards, response and retell exercises |
| Later literary transition | Longer original adapted-register narratives followed only by carefully sourced, legally cleared public-domain or appropriately licensed reading selections |
| Separate advanced course | Book I foundation, Book II developing reader, Book III advanced reader; vocabulary targets and prose length increase instead of front-loading |

## E. What “1,500” and “3,000” must actually mean

- **Counting unit:** An independently reviewed **lexeme/lemma ID**, not an inflected spelling, proper name, word token or quiz option. Forms (*rosa, rosam, rosae*) count as one lexeme; genuinely distinct homographs require separate disambiguated IDs.
- **“Introduced”:** First occurs in an approved Latin reading/dialogue with comprehensible context, a precise gloss or illustration, and at least one checkable encounter. A spreadsheet allocation is **not** introduced vocabulary.
- **“Recycled”:** Additional meaningful appearances in later lectiones and reviews; these do not count again as new.
- **“Recognized”:** Confirmed by a learner in graded contextual and spaced checks, not simply included in a story.
- **“Productive”:** Demonstrated in a voluntary spoken or written response. This is separately tracked.
- **Policy:** **Minimum 1,500 unique introduced lemmas by the end of Book II** and a stretch objective of **3,000+ by the end of Book III**; automatic build should fail to mark those milestones complete if it cannot enumerate unique reviewed lexeme IDs in actual chapter content.

## F. Risk management and pacing

To support a slow beginning, the first 50 chapters still observe the previously chosen **15-new-lemma ceiling**. Instead of weakening that rule, a growing reading series extends the full course to **130 chapters** in three books (50 + 40 + 40). New-lemma budgets increase in later books only alongside longer reading and repeated exposure. See `CURRICULUM_130_MASTER_PLAN.md` and `vocabulary-budgets-130.json`.

A preliminary budget is a **target allocation**, not a complete 3,000-entry curated lexicon and not a claim of student mastery. Before publication, source licensing, lemmatization, homograph disambiguation, macrons, grammar and Roman-history claims need expert review. The app stays static, local-first and free of copyrighted *Familia Romana* text and artwork.
